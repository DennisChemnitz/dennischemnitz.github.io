The conditioned Lyapunov spectrum for random dynamical systems

Matheus M. Castro, Dennis Chemnitz, Hugo Chu, Maximilian Engel, Jeroen S. W. Lamb, Martin Rasmussen

Annales de l’Institut Henri Poincaré, Probabilités et Statistiques 61(3), 1845–1877

PDF ↗DOI ↗arXiv ↗

We establish a full Lyapunov spectrum for random dynamical systems with absorption. Embedding the process conditioned on indefinite survival into the random dynamical systems framework gives convergence of finite-time exponents in conditioned probability.

Characterizing Dynamical Stability of Stochastic Gradient Descent in Overparameterized Learning

Dennis Chemnitz, Maximilian Engel

Journal of Machine Learning Research 26(134), 1–46

PDF ↗Journal ↗arXiv ↗

We characterize which interpolation minima are dynamically stable under gradient descent and stochastic gradient descent. A characteristic Lyapunov exponent determines whether SGD can accumulate at a given minimum, connecting local stability to the selection of solutions.

Positive Lyapunov Exponent in the Hopf Normal Form with Additive Noise

Dennis Chemnitz, Maximilian Engel

Communications in Mathematical Physics 402(2), 1807–1843

PDF ↗DOI ↗arXiv ↗

We prove that sufficiently strong shear produces a positive Lyapunov exponent in the Hopf normal form with additive noise. The proof uses a scaling limit and continuity of Lyapunov exponents to establish shear-induced chaos.

Stochastic Flows with Strong Shear - Part I: Strong Completeness and Set Attractors

Dennis Chemnitz, Maximilian Engel, Michael Scheutzow

arXiv:2610.06321

PDF ↗arXiv ↗

We study how strong, radius-dependent shear affects two-dimensional stochastic flows. The paper gives sufficient and necessary conditions for strong completeness and the existence of set attractors, and shows how sufficiently fast growth of the shear can cause exceptional initial conditions to escape to infinity in finite time.

Mixing at the Batchelor Scale for White-In-Time Flows

Robin Chemnitz, Dennis Chemnitz

arXiv:2512.04297 · Revised June 2026

PDF ↗arXiv ↗

We study passive scalar mixing in smooth, white-in-time flows. Bounds on the almost-sure exponential dissipation rate establish Batchelor-scale behavior in explicit two- and three-dimensional models, including estimates on mixing without diffusion.
